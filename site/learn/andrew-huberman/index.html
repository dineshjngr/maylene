What the evidence can tell us

A published paper can establish what a particular experiment measured. Check the compound identity, formulation, study population, comparator and outcome. A cell or animal result is not a demonstration of human benefit, and a pharmaceutical trial does not validate a retail research product.

Check attribution at the original source

A personal-use claim needs an attributable original statement, with enough context to distinguish the speaker’s own experience from a guest’s comment or hypothetical discussion. A second-hand summary, product photograph or repeated online claim is not sufficient.

  • BPC-157 and Achilles tendon healing — Staresinic et al., 2003

    Rat tendon-injury and cell experiments; this is not a human treatment trial.

  • BPC-157 and growth-hormone receptors — Chang et al., 2014

    Rat tendon fibroblasts; receptor-expression findings do not establish clinical benefit.

  • Semax and BDNF signalling — Dolotov et al., 2006

    Rat hippocampal and behavioural experiments; not proof of cognitive improvement in people.

    Comparative clinical study of 60 patients. The abstract does not establish long-term safety or equivalence to all anxiety treatments.

  • Epithalon and telomerase — Khavinson et al., 2003

    Human fibroblast cultures; a cellular telomere observation does not demonstrate longer human lifespan.
